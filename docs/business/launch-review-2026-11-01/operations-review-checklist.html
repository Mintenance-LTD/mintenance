Incident procedure

  1. Record and contain. Note discovery time, affected feature, users, deployment and provider IDs. Keep beta protection. Pause the affected release/payment operation where needed; preserve logs and avoid repeat charges or deletion of evidence.
  2. Investigate. Compare recent releases, migrations, provider health, webhook state and cron history. Determine whether the issue is outage, money inconsistency, access exposure or a privacy breach. Assign one accountable owner and next update time.
  3. Recover safely. Use an application rollback only after checking database compatibility. Keep financial-history and privacy safeguards. Reconcile Stripe before replaying a financial operation, using its original operation/idempotency identity.
  4. Assess notification. For a personal data breach, assess risk promptly and document the decision. Notify the ICO without undue delay and within 72 hours where notification is required; assess whether affected people need notice. Obtain appropriate incident/legal advice. ICO breach guidance.
  5. Verify and close. Repeat the failed flow, test access controls and callbacks, confirm journals and alerts, communicate the outcome and record prevention work. Do not reopen public access as part of an incident fix.

Refund and dispute procedure

  1. Verify the requesting party and job. Review the agreement, reason, statutory rights, completed work and evidence.
  2. Load the original payment and check already-refunded totals, currency, transfers, refunds in progress and open card disputes. A refund and a Stripe chargeback require coordinated handling.
  3. Record the approved amount and basis; use the authenticated admin flow and required MFA. Do not edit the ledger to force an outcome or create a new charge to cover an exception.
  4. Track submitted → pending → succeeded/failed and reconcile webhook/application state. Retry through recovery using the same operation identity, not a second unrelated refund.
  5. Tell the customer the confirmed status and next update. Retain the minimum evidence needed and meet the relevant refund or dispute deadline.
